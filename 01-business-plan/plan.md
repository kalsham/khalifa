# Business Plan — UGC Creator Business OS

## The product, in one sentence

A spreadsheet toolkit that helps UGC (user-generated content) creators run
their brand-deal business like a business: pipeline tracking, rate
calculation, invoicing, content calendar, and usage-rights tracking in one
file.

## Why this niche (the mentor-note)

Digital products die for one of two reasons: nobody wants them, or everyone
already has ten versions of them. So the filter for picking a niche is
always: **real, provable demand** + **a gap in what's already selling**.

- **Demand is real and provable**: UGC creator is now a defined job — brands
  pay individuals to make native-style content without posting it
  themselves. Search "UGC rate card," "UGC creator tracker," "how much to
  charge for UGC" and you'll find creators actively asking these questions
  with no single good answer, not academic interest.
- **The gap**: Etsy and Gumroad are flooded with generic "freelancer
  planners" and "small business trackers." Almost none of them handle the
  parts specific to UGC work — usage rights/licensing terms per deal (this
  is the #1 thing new UGC creators get wrong and lose money on), whitelisting
  vs. organic usage, and per-deliverable rate math (a video is not priced
  like a static photo).
- **Low build complexity, high perceived value**: it's a spreadsheet, not
  software — I can build the entire real thing today, formulas and all. Perceived
  value is high because it replaces something creators would otherwise pay
  a bookkeeper or lose money not tracking at all.

This is a template for the *method*, not just this one product: pick a
sub-audience underserved by the generic version of an existing product
category, build the specific version, price against the value it replaces
(bookkeeping time, lost income from underpricing) not against generic
template prices.

## The offer

| Tier | What's included | Price | Why this price |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Base** | The spreadsheet (all tabs, Google Sheets + Excel) | $19 | Impulse-buy price point; matches Etsy digital template norms |
| **Pro Bundle** | Spreadsheet + PDF quick-start guide + rate-negotiation script templates + contract clause checklist | $39 | Anchors the base price as a "deal," ~2x AOV from bundling low-marginal-cost extras |
| **Done-for-you setup** (later, not now) | 1:1 15-min Loom walking through their own filled-in copy | $75 | Only add once organic sales prove demand — service doesn't scale, validate product-market fit first |

Start with Base + Pro Bundle only. Don't build the done-for-you tier until
you have at least 20–30 organic sales — it's a distraction from the
product-market-fit question right now.

## Distribution — where this actually sells

Ranked by effort-to-first-sale, cheapest first:

1. **Etsy** (list first) — has built-in buyer intent for "digital planner /
   tracker" searches, near-zero traffic cost to start. This is where you validate
   the offer is right before spending on anything else.
2. **Gumroad** — for linking directly from content (see #3); lower discovery
   but zero marketplace fees comparison advantage and better for owning the
   customer relationship/email list.
3. **Short-form content** (TikTok/Instagram) — the highest-leverage channel
   *for this specific niche* because the audience (UGC creators) already
   lives there. Content angle: "how much I made from X brand deal" /
   "the mistake that cost me $400 on a brand deal" (usage rights) →
   link to the tracker. This is marketing content about the *problem*, not
   an ad for the product — the product is the natural solution mentioned at
   the end.

## Launch sequence

1. Finish product (this session) → test it yourself with fake data end to end
2. List on Etsy with SEO-optimized title/tags (see `listing-copy.md` once built)
3. Mirror listing on Gumroad
4. Post 3–5 short-form videos about UGC pricing/usage-rights mistakes,
   linking to the product in bio — this is the actual growth engine, not the
   marketplace listings alone
5. Iterate pricing/content based on the first 2 weeks of actual sales data —
   don't guess further than that, let real buyers tell you

## What happens after this one product

Once this validates (sells without heavy hand-holding), the repeatable move
is: same method, new sub-niche. Candidates already visible from the same
research: freelance video editors (usage/licensing + turnaround tracking),
newsletter sponsorship trackers (rate cards + sponsor pipeline), or a
"Pro" upsell tier for this same product once you have real customer requests
telling you what tier 2 should include — never guess tier 2 before tier 1 sells.
